## Strongest alternative candidate: historical Sanskrit scan

[Wikimedia Commons hosts a 732-page file](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%E0%A4%B6%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%80%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%A6%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AC%E0%A5%83%E0%A4%B9%E0%A4%A4%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AA%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B6%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%B9%E0%A5%8B%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B6%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A4%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%8D_(%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%AC%E0%A5%8B%E0%A4%A7%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%A8%E0%A5%80%E0%A4%9F%E0%A5%80%E0%A4%95%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B8%E0%A4%B9%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%8D).pdf)
entitled *Śrīmad Bṛhatpārāśarahorāśāstram (with Subodhinī commentary)*.
Its file page identifies 1899, source as “scanned under digitalized sanskrit
corps,” and public-domain tags for **India and the United States**. The
linked [Sanskrit Wikisource index](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%85%E0%A4%A8%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%95%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%A3%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%95%E0%A4%BE:%E0%A4%B6%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%80%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%A6%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AC%E0%A5%83%E0%A4%B9%E0%A4%A4%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AA%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B6%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%B9%E0%A5%8B%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B6%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A4%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%8D_(%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%AC%E0%A5%8B%E0%A4%A7%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%A8%E0%A5%80%E0%A4%9F%E0%A5%80%E0%A4%95%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B8%E0%A4%B9%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%8D).pdf)
labels it 1899, Jnanasagar, Mumbai. Its [unproofread first-page
transcription](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%AA%E0%A5%83%E0%A4%B7%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A0%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%8D:%E0%A4%B6%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%80%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%A6%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AC%E0%A5%83%E0%A4%B9%E0%A4%A4%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AA%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B6%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%B9%E0%A5%8B%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B6%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A4%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%8D_(%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%AC%E0%A5%8B%E0%A4%A7%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%A8%E0%A5%80%E0%A4%9F%E0%A5%80%E0%A4%95%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B8%E0%A4%B9%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%8D).pdf/%E0%A5%A7)
names a commentary and editors; it is not just anonymous ancient verses.

This is stronger than an unidentified web transcription because the scan,
nominal date, publisher, page count, and site-level rights labels are
traceable. **The public-domain tags are claims to verify, not clearance.**
Before use, inspect the scanned title/colophon for the actual printing date
and contributors; establish the relevant commentator/editor death dates or
other basis for expiration in India; check US status of this foreign work;
document the scanner/source and any applicable digitization or access terms;
and specify where training, storage, and distribution will occur. The
[Indian Copyright Act section 22](https://copyright.gov.in/Copyright_Act_1957/chapter_v.html)
generally measures protection of an authored literary work from the author's
death, so an 1899 publication year **alone** does not dispose of named
commentary/editorial rights. The online Wikisource transcription has its own
provenance and licensing questions and is not substituted for the scan.

This older Sanskrit edition is textually different from the Santhanam English
edition; existing research-note locators cannot be transferred to it without
new scholarly checking. It also lacks a ready English/Telugu parallel corpus.
No source text from it was copied into the repository.

## Smallest next action

Have a rights-qualified reviewer examine **the specific Commons scan's
title/colophon and file-history/license evidence** and record a one-page
India/US and digitization-rights decision naming every relevant contributor
and planned training/distribution jurisdiction. If the public-domain basis
cannot be supported, ask Ranjan Publications for a written, edition-specific
AI-training license for the local English volumes. Until one route succeeds,
the manifest must retain both local PDFs as blocked and no training corpus
should be made.
